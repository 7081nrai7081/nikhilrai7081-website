<div class="cs-block">
      <p>Run any live website through a free SEO audit tool and you'll get a score, a list of red and yellow flags, and a strong urge to fix all of them today. Some of that urge is justified. Titles, meta descriptions, canonical tags and broken schema are real, fixable problems, and a good automated check catches them faster than a person scrolling through page source ever could. But a score out of 100 also tends to flatten everything into the same size problem, and that's where most people misread the report. A missing meta description and a genuinely thin content strategy are not the same size problem, even when they show up as two lines in the same list.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cs-block">
      <h2>What an automated audit is actually good at</h2>
      <p>Static checks are fast, cheap and consistent, which makes them exactly right for the mechanical layer of SEO: does every page have one clear title, a real meta description, a canonical tag that points at the right URL, one H1, valid structured data, and images with alt text. None of that requires judgment. It requires someone (or something) to actually look, which is the part most sites skip because it's tedious, not because it's hard. That's the gap a <a href="/free-seo-audit">free instant audit</a> is built to close in about 30 seconds instead of an afternoon of manually opening view-source.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cs-block">
      <h2>What it can't tell you</h2>
      <p>A static check reads the HTML a server hands back. It doesn't know if your title actually matches what people search for, whether your content answers the question someone had when they typed a query, or whether the three competitors outranking you have stronger backlink profiles, better site speed under real user conditions, or just been publishing consistently for four years longer. It also can't see your business: whether the page it just graded is even the right page to be optimizing, or whether your actual bottleneck is traffic you're already getting failing to convert once it lands.</p>
      <p>This is the same gap between a linter and a code review. A linter catches missing semicolons and unused variables in seconds. It has no opinion on whether the architecture makes sense. Both matter. They're not interchangeable, and treating a 100/100 audit score as "SEO is done" is how sites end up technically clean and still invisible.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cs-block">
      <h2>How to actually use a free audit result</h2>
      <div class="cs-steps">
        <div class="cs-step"><span class="num">1</span><div><h3>Fix the critical and high findings first</h3><p>Missing titles, noindex left on by accident, broken canonical tags. These are unambiguous and cheap to fix, so there's no reason to leave them.</p></div></div>
        <div class="cs-step"><span class="num">2</span><div><h3>Treat medium and low findings as a checklist, not a verdict</h3><p>A missing sitemap or a slightly-long meta description won't sink a page on its own. Batch these instead of panicking over the score.</p></div></div>
        <div class="cs-step"><span class="num">3</span><div><h3>Ask what the tool didn't check</h3><p>Search intent match, content depth, site speed under real conditions, backlink quality. If a report doesn't mention these, it isn't measuring them.</p></div></div>
